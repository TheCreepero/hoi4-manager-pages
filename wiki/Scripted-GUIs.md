# Scripted GUIs: the manager pages

> (from the 1440p mod) Copied at the split of 2026-10-21 from the HOI4 1440p UI Mod, whose reworked vanilla screens it still describes beside the pages; a later pass trims it to the pages.

Milestone D of the [Roadmap](Roadmap.md) adds pages of the mod's own beside the vanilla panels: values the game has and shows nowhere in one place, as cards and tables that use the width of a 2560x1440 screen. They are scripted GUIs, the game's way to add widgets that script fills. This page holds the rules for them, what each page shows and how to work on them. What the script language offers is in [Script Reference](Script-Reference.md); what the engine does with a scripted GUI is rows X1 to X70 of [Engine Facts](Engine-Facts.md).

- **Cost:** script lives under `common/` and changes the game's checksum. The mod no longer works with Ironman and achievements, and in multiplayer every player needs it. Decided by the user on 2026-10-12.
- **Planned with the user on 2026-10-12:** all four managers and graphs over time; pages docked to their tab; values of the own country only; a top bar strip later.

## Rules

1. **Show, navigate and keep records.** A scripted button changes its page (hide, sort, choose) or moves the camera. The mod may also write records: its own variables, arrays and flags on a country, a state or a character, and lines the game keeps for show, such as an entry in a division's history. A record is written on the player's click or by a hook the player can switch off. Not allowed: giving the player what the game charges for (a unit, a building, equipment, a skill level, a trait, a medal's bonus without its price). `tests/ScriptedGuis.Tests.ps1` holds the list of commands the mod's script may use.
   - Until 2026-10-12 the rule was "never change the game". The user loosened it for the role-play pages ([Roleplay Pages](Roleplay-Pages.md), which gives the reasons the old rule had). The four pages built so far write nothing but their own variables.
2. **Own country only.** Every scripted GUI is `player_context`. Script could read any country's real numbers, also where the game's intelligence system hides them; the mod does not.
3. **Every value is checked against the game's own number** before a page ships, and the check is logged in the phase notes. A name does not always mean what it seems to: `num_armies` counts divisions, `max_available_manpower_k` is not the free manpower.
4. **The map is one key away.** Shift+M hides and shows the page of the open tab. A page remembers per country whether it is open.
5. **No loop in anything drawn every tick.** Counting runs on a click, once a day or once a month; a long list is updated through `dirty`.
6. **Vanilla content only.** Equipment kinds, buildings, resources and modifiers are named in the mod's files, so what another mod adds does not appear. Tank rows use the chassis of No Step Back.
7. **Names** carry the prefix `mod1440_` (windows, files, variables, effects), sprites `GFX_1440p_SG_*`, and the art is vanilla sprites and textures.
8. **The AI never looks:** `ai_enabled = { always = no }` on every one.

### How the manager pages look

Reworked on 2026-10-12 at the user's request ("a similar UI polishing pass on every other feature"), in the look the Records got the same day. The user installed the build the next morning, played with it and wrote: "Overall I'm very happy with the results." A cold start on 2026-10-13 then showed all four as the renderer had drawn them (`reference/mod/`); what a new game of 1936 does not show is listed under each page.

- **Tiles for figures.** What a page counts stands on plates, a small label over a large value, in a band across the top of the page.
- **Tables under a bar of column heads**, every second row on a darker band, the chosen row on a lighter one.
- **Shares as bars**, with the percentage beside them; a bar below 75% is orange (the texture of the fleet's strength bar).
- **Grey for what does not apply**: a kind of equipment the country neither has nor needs, a modifier that changes nothing.
- **The game's own icons** in a text: the three factories, the seven resources.
- The helpers are in `tools/build_managers.py` under "the look every page shares" (`tile`, `heads`, `stripes`, `listing`, `card`, `bar_sprite`); the Records pages in `tools/build_records.py` use the same ones. The tool stops when a page names a sprite that `interface/1440p_ui.gfx` does not declare.

### Army Readiness

File `interface/mod1440_army_readiness.gui`, docked 975 px wide beside the template panel, from under the top bar to above the army portraits. It stops short of the theatre box under the clock.

- **Two forms** since the redesign of 2026-10-16: the whole page, or Summary, its title strip and figures only (975x244), so the map below is free for picking a deploy location. They are two scripted GUIs on the tab, the head and the body. Shift+M hides and shows the page in the form it was left in; the Summary and Full button switches the form.
- **Top:** the date of the last count, Count again and Summary in the title strip; twelve tiles in two cards, Manpower and Army, the last a Fuel tile with a bar.
- **Equipment:** a list of the kinds in use only, worst equipped first, each with the game's picture of the kind: in stock, in the field, needed, short (red when the stock cannot fill the gap, orange when it can), and a bar with its percentage. The kinds are every archetype a land sub-unit needs, from a table made from the game (`tools/equipment_kinds_table.py`) or, for a playset with a map of its own, from the playset (`tools/equipment_kinds_mods_table.py`), chosen in script by the number of states.
- **Armies:** one row of 50 px per commander with troops: portrait, rank and skill, the state of his HQ as a button that moves the camera, divisions and how many are infantry, armour, motorised and mechanised, fighting with attack and defence, planning as a bar, and the worst equipped kind as a picture with its share.

| Value | Script name | Checked against |
|---|---|---|
| Free manpower | `manpower_k` | the top bar (1332.6 for 1.33M) |
| In training | `amount_manpower_in_deployment_queue` | rose by 9600 per infantry division queued |
| In the army, navy, air force | `deployed_army_manpower_k` and its two siblings | not compared with a vanilla number |
| Garrisons need | `garrison_manpower_need` | 0 in a 1936 start: not seen above 0 |
| Divisions, battalions | `num_divisions`, `num_battalions` | the army's 30 divisions |
| Under strength (below 75%), low organisation (below 50%) | counted by `mod1440_ar_count` with `unit_strength` and `unit_organization` | 0 in a 1936 start: the loop counted right with other limits (X22) |
| Stock, in the field, needed, per kind | `num_equipment@`, `num_equipment_in_armies@`, `num_target_equipment_in_armies@` | the stock column of the logistics tab; the Soviet Union's shortages show red with bars around 76% |
| Short, per kind | needed less in the field, worked out by `mod1440_ar_count` | empty for every kind in Germany's 1936, where needed and in the field are equal (24900 infantry equipment, 890 support equipment, 348 artillery, 605 trucks, 720 light tanks). The Soviet Union: 27033 of 114750 infantry equipment, 170 of 570 support equipment, 218 of 1032 artillery, 147 of 1750 trucks and 1000 of 3300 light tanks, each what needed less in the field gives on the page (rounded up since that check, X65), with the bars orange at 70% and 69% |
| Commander: rank | `is_field_marshal`, `is_corps_commander` | the same triggers mark the rank on the Officers page |
| Commander: skill, divisions, battalions | `skill_level`, `num_units`, `num_battalions` in the commander's scope | the army badge |
| Commander: fighting, planning, by kind | `num_units_in_combat`, `avg_unit_planning_ratio`, `num_infantry`, `num_armored`, `num_motorized` | 26, 3 and 1 of Germany's 30 divisions; the first two not seen above 0 |
| Commander: equipment | the lowest of have / need over the sixteen kinds, worked out by `mod1440_ar_count` | 100% for a fully equipped army |

- **Live and counted.** Texts that hold a value of the game follow it at once, also while paused. The two division counts, what is short, the bars and the list of commanders are counted: at the start of a game for every country, each day for a player, and on Count again. The date under the button says when.
- **Not in the numbers:** divisions in training. "Needed" is what the divisions in the field ask for (queueing two divisions left it unchanged).
- **Not listed:** an army without a commander. Script reaches armies through their generals only.

### States

File `interface/mod1440_states.gui`, docked 1472 px wide beside the building column of Construction.

- **Columns** in five groups with a gap between them: free slots; infrastructure, civilian and military factories, dockyards, refineries, fuel silos, air bases, anti-air, radar; oil, aluminium, rubber, tungsten, steel, chromium, coal; population; compliance and resistance for a state that is not a core. A cell that would read 0 is left empty. Free slots are green.
- **The choices** (All, Cores, Occupied, With free slots, Refresh) stand in the title strip; a caption names the buildings, the resources and the occupation columns.
- **Totals:** a bar under the list sums every column over the states that are listed, so a choice (Cores, With free slots) changes it. Worked out when the list is made.
- **A state that is not a core** has its name in another colour.
- **Free slots** are found with the trigger `free_building_slots` for a civilian factory, asked five times per state (16 or more? 8 or more? ...), because the trigger takes a written number only (X20). Checked: Hessen read 6, and its state view said 9 unlocked slots with 3 buildings. The state's `modifier@local_building_slots` would have said 8.
- **The list is made** when the page opens, on a sort, a choice or Refresh, and each day while it is open. The numbers of a row are from that moment (`dirty`). The arrow of the sorted column is placed then too: the game's small arrow at about half its size, on the upper right corner of the head (since 2026-10-13; at its own size it stood over the text of a narrow head). The group captions stand 5 px higher since then, clear of it. Seen in the game on 2026-10-13.
- **Sorting:** a click on a column head sorts largest first, a second click turns the order, the State head gives the game's order back. 152 Soviet states sort in a few milliseconds (X22).
- **Checked on 2026-10-13:** the totals against their columns for the 24 states of Germany's start (free slots 95, infrastructure 81, civilian factories 35 as the top bar has them, military 28, dockyards 10), the bands and the group captions.
- **Not seen in game:** the Occupied choice and the two occupation columns with real values (a 1936 start has no occupied state); forts and naval bases, which stand in provinces and are left out.
- **Redesign of 2026-10-16 (built, not seen in game):** the table in a card of 26 rows over a band of four cards for the chosen state; a left click chooses, a right-click or Show on map moves the camera and hides the page; slot bars that count the game's own shared buildings only; the owner's flag for a state held but not owned; damaged levels in red; resources rounded. What was built and what is open: [D3 notes](phases/D3-states.md), "Redesign build".

### National Figures

File `interface/mod1440_figures.gui`, one page of 1680x1210 docked to `politics_tab` beside the politics panel (since 2026-10-16; before, National Figures and History were two pages of a window of their own). The build of 2026-10-16 is described in the [D4 notes](phases/D4-national-figures.md), "Redesign build"; what follows down to "History" is the page as it was checked on 2026-10-13, and its checks still hold for the values.

- **Since 2026-10-16:** twenty tiles across the top (the ten below, political power and command power with their daily gain, stability, war support and fuel with bars, free manpower, manpower in the field, divisions, infantry equipment, casualties), each a button that draws its figure in the graph under them; the party bars went (the panel beside the page shows the parties). The modifiers stand in four scrolling lists made by `mod1440_nf_fill`: What applies (default) or All modifiers.
- **Today, across the top:** ten tiles (civilian factories with how many are free for construction, military factories and dockyards with how many have no production line, research slots, technologies, states, convoys, trains, ships, planes), and under them stability, war support, fuel and the four parties as bars.
- **Modifiers** in eight cards (Politics, Manpower, Occupation and Diplomacy, Industry, Army, Research and Intelligence, Navy, Air Force) come from `modifier@<name>`: the sum the game itself works with, over every law, spirit, advisor, technology and focus. A modifier of 0 has a grey name and no value, so what applies stands out. A gain is green and a loss red; for costs and penalties (consumer goods, supply consumption, training time, resistance) it is the other way round.
- **Labels** are the game's own modifier names for 90 of the 101 modifiers (`MODIFIER_<NAME>`), so they are the words of the tooltips. All 101 names are in the game's `modifiers_documentation.md`.
- **The bars** are filled by `mod1440_nf_bars`, when the page opens and each day; the numbers beside them follow the game by themselves.
- **Checked:** party popularity against the politics panel (40, 35, 10, 15); factories against the top bar; consumer goods (25.0% and -2.4%, which is the 15 factories the construction tab shows); conscription 2.5% and resources to market 25% against the two laws of the start; the resource table against the resource row of the logistics tab, all seven. The other modifiers are the game's sums under the game's names and were not compared one by one.
- **Checked on 2026-10-13:** the bars against the numbers beside them (stability 81.0%, war support 35.0%, fuel 297K of 371K, the parties 40, 35, 10, 15), the tiles against the top bar (35, 28 and 10 factories) and the resource table once more (3, 104, -5, 3, 177, 1, 195). Of the eight modifiers of Occupation and Diplomacy three have a value for Germany in 1936 (compliance growth -2.0%, resistance target +5.0%, surrender limit -9.0%) and five stand in grey; they were not compared with a tooltip of the game.

### History

In the same file. Since 2026-10-16 it is the middle of the National Figures page: the tiles choose the figure, the graph spans the page (120 columns of 10 px on 12 px steps) and six plates give the latest month, the change in a month and in a year, the highest, the lowest and the change since the first month. Before, the History and Figures buttons switched between two pages.

- **What is written down:** free manpower, manpower in the field, divisions, the three kinds of factories, stability, war support, fuel, infantry equipment in stock, casualties, political power; since 2026-10-16 also research slots, technologies, command power, states, convoys, trains, ships and planes. A save of the build before has the first twelve: the eight new ones get -1 for the months before, which draws no column and counts for nothing.
- **When:** at the start of a game for every country, then on the first of each month for a player. After ten years the oldest month is dropped. A game that gets the mod after its start has no column until the first of the next month, and the page says so.
- **The table** on the left lists the twelve figures with the newest month and the change in a month and in a year (green for a rise, red for a fall; for casualties the other way round). A click on a figure draws it.
- **The graph** is a row of 120 columns, each a progress bar that script fills to the month's share of the highest month (X17), on four bands that mark the quarters of the scale. The tooltip of a column gives its date and value. Above it: the newest, the highest and the lowest month, the change since the first month, and the number of months.
- **Zoom in** lets the columns start at the lowest month instead of at zero, so that a small change shows; the scale on the left follows.
- **Years:** each record now carries its year (`global.year`), and the year stands under the first column of each year. The marks are ten texts of the page that script moves, as the arrow of a sorted column (X4). Months written down before 2026-10-12 have no year and get no mark.
- **Left open in a saved game**, the page is drawn again within a day: it is drawn only when its `dirty` variable changes (X16), and the daily hook now changes it while the page is open.
- **Vanilla has a history of its own for one thing:** the logistics tab draws the stock of an equipment kind over time. The twelve figures here have none.
- **Checked on 2026-10-13** with the one month of a new game (the table, free manpower 1332.6 as Army Readiness has it, the band of the chosen row, the five tiles, the scale and its bands) and with made-up months (three effects of `tools/check_effects.py` fill the arrays: `effect mod1440_chk_months`, `_old`, `_few`; on that day they were `mod1440_h_test` and its two siblings): 120 columns with the year marks 1937 to 1946, the changes in a month and in a year in green and red (for casualties the other way round), Zoom in with the scale from the lowest month, seven columns, and months without years as an older save has them (no marks, nothing else amiss).
- **Not seen in a check:** a month written by `on_monthly` with its year (`global.year`). It runs in the user's game.

### Top bar strip

File `interface/mod1440_top_bar.gui`, four scripted GUIs attached to `top_bar` (X8), so they stand on every screen. The free part of the vanilla bar at 2560x1440 runs from the last value box (x 968, or the nukes counter at 1026 for a country with bombs) to the clock plate at x 2168; the strip uses x 1042 to 2152 and stays on the band's flat interior (y 3 to 31), above its dark lower edge and the alerts row. The user's one rule for it (2026-10-16): nothing overflows the top bar.

- **Chips.** Each value is the game's own value box (`generic_box_smallest.dds` as a cornered tile, 26 px at y 4), the game's icon and the number in `hoi_18mbs` with a grey unit (`/d`, `/wk`, `free`, `idle`); the label is the tooltip. Every text has a fixed width measured for its widest plausible value, so a larger number is cut with dots inside its chip. Idle military factories and dockyards are yellow while above zero; a resource balance below zero is red with the game's crossed icon.
- **The defaults**, left to right: political power and command power per day, stability and war support per week, civilian factories with free for construction, military factories and dockyards with idle, fuel in store of capacity: the numbers the vanilla bar keeps in its tooltips. They end at x 1951; the edit button stands at 1970 and the Records handle at 2002 to 2152.
- **Chosen in the game** (user, 2026-10-17). The edit button opens a panel under the bar (842x544 at 1310,40) with eighteen values in five groups (gains, factories, fuel, manpower, resources), each with its icon, name, width and a switch; a bar of the width used of 912 px; Default; close with Escape. A switch that would not fit is refused (`_click_enabled`, X14) with a red width. The chips stand in the order of the list and close up: each widget's `x` and visibility follow country variables (X4) that `mod1440_tb_place` sets on a click. No ordering by the player (user, 2026-10-17).
- **Kept with the country.** The choice is `mod1440_tb_on_<n>` and the places `mod1440_tb_x_*` on the country; `on_startup` sets the defaults for the player's countries, and a save of an earlier build shows a fixed twin of the eight until the first click on the edit button or the next day.
- **No loop, no `dirty`:** the chips read game variables (`political_power_daily`, `modifier@stability_weekly`, `num_of_available_military_factories`, `fuel_k` and the like, all in the game's `documentation/`); only the placement effect runs, on a click.
- **Checked:** by render and tests, then by the user in their own game on 2026-10-17 ("it works well"). No harness check ran; the values were not compared one by one with the tooltips in a check, and `reference/mod/` has no capture of the strip yet.

### The Records

File `interface/mod1440_records.gui`, written by `tools/build_records.py`: a window of 1680x1220 at 440,100 with four pages, a button in the top bar (Shift+J), and a card beside the vanilla state view. Why the pages are what they are, and what script can reach for them: [Roleplay Pages](Roleplay-Pages.md). They keep a record from the day the mod is first active in a game.

- **How the pages look** (polished on 2026-10-12 at the user's request, "really polished and fit their purpose"): dark cards as on the manager pages. A figure is a tile, a small label over a large value on a plate. Every list has a bar of column heads and every second row a darker band; the chosen officer's row a lighter one. Names stand in the game's highlight yellow, countries with their flag, dates written out ("1 January, 1936"). A line of the diary starts with the game's text icon of its family. Medals are the game's large medal strips, side by side on an officer's file. Party support and an enemy's surrender are bars. The sprites (`GFX_1440p_SG_tile`, `_stripe_*`, `_pick_*`, `_bar_wide`) are tiles of vanilla textures, declared in `interface/1440p_ui.gfx`.
- **Portraits, flags, officers on the lines and the file of a war (2026-10-13).** The user, after playing with the build: "I just want more graphical details", and three wishes: an officer's portrait in his file and in the list; a line in the officer's record and in the War Diary when a battle over a victory point is won or lost and when a state is captured; a file for a war as there is one for an officer, with the flags and leaders of each side. **Checked in the game on 2026-10-13**, in a live session and from a cold start (rows X58 to X64 of [Engine Facts](Engine-Facts.md); the log is in the [E1 notes](phases/E1-war-diary.md), "Game checks").
  - **A portrait** is an icon whose sprite script names: the `image` property of a scripted GUI takes a text, and the text can hold a variable. The game declares a sprite for each named character, and script can ask whether a character is a certain one (`is_character`), so the mod carries the list of who has which sprite (`tools/portraits_table.py`, 1619 characters, made by `tools/make_game_tables.py`) and leaves the sprite's name on the officer as a text key, once. A general the game made up has a portrait script cannot learn: he shows the game's picture of an unknown leader. Portraits stand in every row of the Officers list (34x46), in the officer's file, on the Government page (the head of government, with the country's flag) and in the file of a war.
  - **A flag** is an icon whose image is `[<country>.GetFlag]`, on the game's sprite of a flag in its frame.
  - **A line has an officer.** When a state changes hands the line names the general who had the most divisions in it, and the same line shows in his file. A line now holds up to three scopes: what it is about, a country, the officer.
  - **Victory points.** A hook tells of a state, nothing tells of a province. So once a day, while the country is at war, script looks at who holds each of the game's 1495 victory points (`global.province_controllers`, the list in `tools/victory_points_table.py`) and compares with its copy of the day before. A point that passed between the country and an enemy becomes a line "Victory point taken" or "Victory point lost", with the point's name, the enemy and the general on the spot; the war and the general each count it. A point worth 15 or more stands in the main log too. A point that changes hands because its country capitulated or is gone gets no line. **A battle that was won without the place changing hands leaves no line:** the battle hooks say who won, not where.
  - **All means all.** The All view of the War Diary goes through both logs together, by date. Before, the single states stood under the Territory filter only (the user's choice of 2026-10-12, which this wish replaced).
  - **The file of a war** opens on a click on its row, as an officer's does: the two flags and names, the two heads of government with portrait, party and ideology, since when and until when, six figures (days, casualties, states and victory points taken and lost), how close each side is to giving up, and the lines the War Diary has about this enemy.
  - **A game saved before this** has logs and war rows without the new parts: script fills them with zeros when it first touches them.
  - **Fixed on the way:** an office (chief of the army and so on) whose holder was replaced on the same day, or who came back after a vacancy, was not written down. The test was `NOT = { A B }`, which the game reads as "neither A nor B".
- **Officers the game gives no number (2026-10-13).** The user tested with other mods: "generals added by mods don't show up in the list", with the mod "Add USA Generals AI REWORK", whose event gives 73 characters their command. A leader whose command comes by an effect has no number, and neither `THIS` nor the game's arrays of leaders hold him (X66), so the list, built from those arrays, passed him by. The same is true of every general a focus of the game makes that way.
  - **He is kept by his place.** A variable holds a character who is no unit leader as his place in the game's list of characters, and that place leads to a unit leader too (X67). So the officer gets a mark, script enters every place until it meets the mark, and the place is written on him (`mod1440_ch_at`). `mod1440_of_me` gives what a variable can hold of the officer in scope: his number, or his place. The Officers list, the row click, the armies list of Army Readiness, the general on the spot and the hooks all go through it, and officers are collected with the game's own loops (`every_army_leader`, `every_navy_leader`).
  - **A place can go stale.** The game reads its character files in the order of their names, so an update or another set of mods moves everybody behind the change. Each character the mod keeps by place carries his place on himself, also the advisors and heads of government on the lines of the diary and in the offices of the Government page (they were kept by place all along). Once a day the character at the highest place the mod keeps is asked whether that is still his. If not, every place is entered once, everybody who carries a mark is found again, and the logs, the list of officers, the chosen officer and the offices are put right (`mod1440_ch_remap`). A person who is gone leaves his line without a name.
  - **No hook tells of him**, so the daily round takes an officer it meets for the first time as commissioned on that day. The first eight of a day get a line each, the others share one: "More officers commissioned". A game saved by an earlier build takes the officers it missed without a date and without lines.
  - **Their portraits.** `python -I tools/make_game_tables.py --mods` reads the characters of the mods in the launcher's playset into `tools/portraits_mods_table.py` (858 characters of five mods on 2026-10-13: Add German, Soviet and USA Generals AI REWORK, the RT56 standalone, USA Reimagined). The build adds them to the lookup. In a game without those mods the names are not found and nothing is logged (X68). The file is optional: without it such an officer shows the unknown leader.
  - **Checked in game** with that mod loaded: three launches on 2026-10-13, the last from a cold start with the mod's event and a daily round behind it ([E2 notes](phases/E2-officer-records.md), checks 5 to 7).
- **The War Diary has the record in figures beside it:** since when it is kept, and fourteen counts (wars begun and ended, capitulations, annexations, states, victory points and capitals, changes of government, officers commissioned, promoted and decorated, divisions cited). Each kind of line has a counter of its own (`mod1440_ct`), so the figures do not shrink when old lines leave the log.
- **How a line is kept:** a date, a text key and up to three scopes (what it is about: a state, a victory point or a person; a country; the officer on the spot). The name is looked up when the row is drawn, so a retired general and an annexed country keep theirs (X34), and a state shows the name its holder of today gives it.
- **Two logs of 1000 lines** (500 until 2026-10-16): the main one, and one for single states and victory points so that an offensive does not push the wars out. A capital and a victory point worth 15 or more are in both; the All view shows such a line once.
- **What a hook tells and what a comparison finds:** a war declared, an annexation, a state changing hands, a promotion, a skill level and a new officer come from hooks at the moment (X43). The end of a war, a capitulation, a subject, the faction and every office are found once a day by comparing with the day before; such a line carries the date of the day that ended (X53).

- **Decorations are a record only.** They use the game's medals of the player's country for their names and icons, cost nothing and give nothing.

## What cannot be shown or done

- **No list of templates, training lines, production lines, the construction queue, the research queue, air wings or fleets.** Script has no array of any of them.
- **No order of the player.** No effect queues a division or a building, sets a priority or a deploy location. Effects that put a unit or a building on the map skip its cost: cheats, which rule 1 keeps out.
- **A row cannot select its army or its state.** `goto_state` moves the camera; the click on the map stays with the player.
- **A page cannot know which tab is open.** A docked page shows with its tab because it is a child of that tab; a window of its own cannot close when its tab does.

## Working on a page

- **Who does what:** a page goes through the stages of [Workflow](Workflow.md) like a screen: the designer proposes it, the user approves the design page, `builder-script` changes the generator and the game checker brings the page up. The rest of this section is what each of them uses.
- **Two tables of game content** stand beside the tools: every character's portrait sprite and every victory point (`tools/portraits_table.py`, `tools/victory_points_table.py`). `python -I tools/make_game_tables.py` makes them from the installed game; run it after a game update. A test fails when they differ from the game.
- **One tool writes everything:** `python -I tools/build_managers.py`. The tables at the top of each section of that file are the pages: change them there. `--check` fails when the files on disk differ, and the test suite runs it. The Records window is in `tools/build_records.py`, which the tool imports; the game's medals are a table in `tools/unit_medals_table.py`, made with `tools/list_medals.py` and to be made again after a game update.
- **Time in a check:** since 2026-10-12 a check may run up to ten days inside January 1936, with a copy of the user's `autosave.hoi4` made first. How to set the speed and read the clock: [Game Recipes](Game-Recipes.md), "Letting time run".
- **Effects for a check:** `python -I tools/check_effects.py` writes throwaway effects that cause events while paused, run the mod's rounds, make a game look as an earlier build or another set of mods left it, and fill History with made-up months ([Game Recipes](Game-Recipes.md), "Effects for a check").
- **With another mod:** a page that reads characters, leaders, states or equipment is checked once with a mod that adds some ([Game Recipes](Game-Recipes.md), "With another mod loaded").
- **Live:** start the game once with `tools\Invoke-GameSteps.ps1 -Start`, change the tool, run it, and the page is redrawn a few seconds later: script, texts and layout (X23). `error.log` gets "Reloading Database" and any parse error. A new `.gui` file and a new sprite need a restart (X27). So does a new text key that script holds as a token: its text stays empty until then. A new scripted effect called from an earlier one in the same file is an "Unknown effect-type" at the first reload and known from the second on (X70).
- **Reading a value without a text box:** `log = "... [?variable]"` in an effect writes to `game.log`.
- **Running an effect without a button:** the console command `effect <name of a scripted effect>`, with nothing selected. The console runs it in the scope of what is selected: an army's general, a state, or with nothing selected the country (X49).
- **Scenes:** the tool also writes `tools/scenes/<page>.json` with a sample for every value, so `python -I tools/render_gui.py --scene <page>` draws the page and `tools\Invoke-GameCheck.ps1 <page>` brings it up and finds its buttons.
- **Tests:** `tests/ScriptedGuis.Tests.ps1`. Files up to date; every effect, trigger and property names a widget that exists; only listed commands; `player_context`; no AI; every name after `@` listed as a token; every text key defined.

## Known gaps

- **Checked on 2026-10-13.** The rework of the four manager pages (2026-10-12) and the additions to the Records (2026-10-13) went through two live checks and a cold start that day (eleven scenes, `reference/mod/` stored again, no line of `error.log` names the mod). The second live check took a new game's newer arrays away, as a save of an older build lacks them: they were padded when the Records opened, and the pages read as before. Still unseen: a month written by the real monthly hook with its year; a real save of an older build; the user's whole playset of 56 mods (the checks loaded this mod alone, or with "Add USA Generals AI REWORK"); a commander without a number in the armies list of Army Readiness and as the general on the spot (the same script as the Officers list, which was seen); a real change of the game's character files, which was made up by effect. `reference/mod/states*` was stored before the group captions were raised by 5 px.
- **The daily and monthly hooks have run in the user's game, not in a check.** No check runs time: the user's autosave is monthly, and a check that let a month pass would overwrite it. `on_startup` was seen in checks (the counts and the first column are there on 1 January 1936). After playing with the mod the user reported on 2026-10-12 that everything works, "including the history ticks": History gets its monthly columns. The daily count was not named on its own; its sign is that the date beside Count again is the day's.
- **Not seen above zero:** garrison need, divisions below strength or organisation, divisions fighting, planning, occupied states.
- **The Records, not seen:** a battle won or lost (the four battle hooks), a capitulation, a landing, an ace, a general who dies in the field, and anything after a save and a load: an officer's record rests on variables kept on the officer, which were seen to last through a session, a promotion and a retirement, not through a save. The hooks of a landing, an airborne landing and an ace write a line to `game.log` ("MOD1440 listening") when they fire, so a game in which they happen shows whether they work.
- **Shift and control clicks** are not used; the harness cannot make them.
- **Other mods:** equipment, buildings, medals and laws they add do not appear (rule 6); a law of another mod reads "another law", because a condition on an idea the game does not know logs an error (X71). A mod that replaces the Recruit & Deploy window takes the docking of Army Readiness with it. A mod with a map of its own needs its victory points in `tools/victory_points_mods_table.py`, or a fifth of the lines name the wrong state (X72). Portraits a mod gives as a file and not as a sprite show the unknown leader. Checked with the user's playset on 2026-10-13: [Compatibility](Compatibility.md).
