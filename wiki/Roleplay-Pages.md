# Role-play pages: the Records

Milestone E of the [Roadmap](Roadmap.md). The manager pages of Milestone D show what the country has today. These pages keep a record of the campaign as it is played: what happened and when, who commands, who governs, which wars were fought. They are scripted GUIs like the manager pages and follow [Scripted GUIs](Scripted-GUIs.md), with one rule changed (below).

- **Asked for by the user on 2026-10-12:** "I like to treat Hoi4 as a role-playing game. That means giving accurate names to units, keeping track of characters."
- **Status:** built on 2026-10-12 (version 1.6) and checked in game; awaiting the tester's judgement. What each page shows and how each value was checked is in [Scripted GUIs](Scripted-GUIs.md), "The Records". Each page has its plan and its notes under `phases/`. The probe ([E0](phases/E0-roleplay-probe.md)) ran on 2026-10-12 and answered fifteen of its seventeen questions; the plans are corrected by it, and what it taught is rows X32 to X49 of [Engine Facts](Engine-Facts.md).
- **Still unseen:** what needs a battle, an air war or a save ("Still open" below).

## The changed rule

Rule 1 of the manager pages was "show and navigate, never change the game". It was a caution proposed with the first plan, not a limit of the engine. Its reasons: the Workshop text could promise that the pages only show; effects that put a unit or a building on the map skip its cost, which is a cheat; in multiplayer an effect of a scripted button is a real action of that player; and a page that only reads cannot harm a save.

The user loosened it on 2026-10-12. The rule now reads:

> **Show, navigate and keep records.** A scripted button changes its page or moves the camera. The mod may also write records: its own variables, arrays and flags (`mod1440_*`) on a country, a state or a character, and lines the game itself keeps for show, such as an entry in a division's history. A record is written on a click of the player or by a hook the player can switch off. Not allowed: giving the player what the game charges for (a unit, a building, equipment, a skill level, a trait, a medal's bonus without its price).

The last sentence is the part of the old rule kept, as a proposal: the user can strike it too. `tests/ScriptedGuis.Tests.ps1` still holds the list of commands the mod's script may use; a command that writes to the game goes into a list of its own there, with the page that needs it.

## What script cannot reach

- **No typing.** The scripted GUI format has buttons, icons, texts and lists. No field takes text, so a page cannot rename anything or hold a note of the player.
- **No stored text.** A variable holds a number, a date, a scope or a text key. A record stores the general, the state or the country, and the name is looked up when the row is drawn. A retired general and an annexed country still give their names (X34).
- **No name of a division, nothing of a ship.** The documentation gives a division no text of its own and ships no scope, and the probe read an empty text for `[THIS.GetName]` in a division's scope (X45). Accurate unit names stay with the reworked vanilla screens (longer name fields) and the user's namelist mods.
- **A portrait for a named character only.** No text function returns one. But an icon's image is a sprite name chosen by script, the game declares a sprite for each named character, and script can ask whether a character is a certain one: so the mod carries the list of who has which sprite (`tools/portraits_table.py`) and the pages show faces since 2026-10-13 (the user's wish; until then this line read "No portrait"). A general the game made up keeps the picture of an unknown leader: his portrait is drawn at random and nothing reads it.
- **No hook** for a finished focus or research, a lost division or ship, and none that says where or against whom a battle was won. For victory points the place is found another way since 2026-10-13: script reads once a day who holds each of them and writes down the ones that passed between the country and an enemy. A battle won without the place changing hands still leaves only a count.
- **No "casualties inflicted".** The documentation has no such value; a country's own casualties are all there is.
- **From a general to his divisions:** no effect goes that way. A division is reached through its country or the state it stands in.
- **From install day.** A record starts when the mod is first active in a game.

## The Records window

One window holds the role-play pages, because none of them belongs to a vanilla tab.

- **Opened by** a button in the free part of the top bar (`parent_window_token = top_bar`, X8) and by Shift+J. Vanilla uses Shift with B, D, E, K, Q, S, W and X; the mod with C, F, G, M, N, O, P, R and U. The probe's button stood at 1300,4 and Shift+J opened and closed its windows (X48).
- **The window** is a scripted GUI without a parent (X7), about 1680x1220 at 440,100: above the vanilla panels, below vanilla pop-ups. Escape closes it before the open tab (X9). The probe's windows filled this rectangle over the map at 68 frames per second.
- **The pages** are scripted GUIs of their own on the same spot, shown by a page number, because a nested container cannot be hidden (X15). This is how National Figures and History share a window today.
- **Files:** `interface/mod1440_records.gui`, `common/scripted_guis/mod1440_records.txt`, written by `tools/build_managers.py` like every page.
- **The other choice:** each page docked to a tab, as the user chose for the managers. Only Government has a tab (Politics), and that window clips (X6). So the plan is one window; the user decides.

## The recording

Built in E1 and used by every page.

- **The log** is five arrays of the player's country with the same index: `mod1440_ev_date` (a `global.date`, for the text), `mod1440_ev_day` (a `global.num_days`, for counting days), `mod1440_ev_kind` (a text key), `mod1440_ev_a` and `mod1440_ev_b` (a state, a country, a character, or nothing). 500 lines; the oldest is dropped. State changes go into a second log of the same form (`mod1440_tr_*`, 500 lines), so that a large offensive does not push the wars and promotions out.
- **One effect writes:** a hook sets three temporary variables and calls `mod1440_ev_add`. Hooks run for every country in the world, so each starts with `is_ai = no` on the country that gets the line.
- **A page shows a copy.** `mod1440_ev_list` copies the lines that pass the page's filter, newest first, into arrays of the page; the list is drawn from the copy by row index (X30) and updated through `dirty` (X16). The States page works this way.
- **Three things are found by comparing, not by a hook:** the end of a war, a new or lost subject, and a change of office. Once a day the mod compares `enemies`, `subjects`, `country_leader` and the advisor arrays with its copy of yesterday. The loops are a few elements long.
- **Size:** about 5000 numbers per human country in a save, beside the 1560 of History. Filling 500 lines and copying a view of 200 from them took no measurable time in the probe (X48).
- **In a row that is scoped** to a general or a country, an array of the player's country is written with `ROOT.` before its name (X36).

## Checking without running time

A check stays paused on 1 January 1936 and saves nothing, or runs at most ten days with the autosave copied first (GEMINI.md, "Ten Days At Most, Never Save"). The game of a check is thrown away, so its state may be changed by effects from the console.

- **Paused, by effects:** a declared war, a state changing hands, an annexation, a promotion, a new general, a retired general, hired advisors, a history entry. The probe did all of these; each fired its hook at once. The console runs an effect in the scope of whatever is selected, so a check deselects first (X49).
- **Needs time:** a battle won or lost, a real conquest, a capitulation by fighting, a landing, an ace, the daily comparison. Two ways: the user plays, and the hooks write a line to `game.log` until seen; or a check may run a few days inside January 1936, which is a decision for the user (below).
- **Needs a save:** whether a record kept on a general, and a general held by the country, come back after loading.

## Still open after the probe

1. **An ace:** whether one can be kept by the country, and what a dead ace's texts show. `add_ace` does not fire the hook, so only air combat answers it. Until then [E5](phases/E5-aces-roll.md) is two counters.
2. **The battle hooks** of generals and admirals: that they fire, for whom, how often. A check ran nine days of a war with Poland and no battle was fought (X55).
3. **Capitulation, landings, a subject made in a peace conference:** their hooks were not triggered.
4. **A save and a load** of an officer's record.
5. **A general who dies** in the field, as against one retired.
6. **When a division takes its next history entry** after one: the next day, or later.
7. **Operatives** in a list: Germany has none at a start.

## Decisions for the user

Answered on 2026-10-12: 1 a check may run up to ten days inside January 1936, with a copy of the autosave made first (not the save of its own); 2 keep "nothing the game charges for"; 3 the window; 4 as proposed; 5 the game's own national medals; 6 "by itself"; 7 yes, 1.6. Not answered: whether a citation should offer a medal; it does, as planned, until the user says otherwise. The questions as they were asked:

1. **The rule as worded above**, with "nothing the game charges for" kept.
2. **One Records window opened from the top bar**, or pages docked to tabs.
3. **Time and saves in a check.** Three things would close most of "Still open": (a) a check that runs up to ten days inside January 1936, with a copy of `autosave.hoi4` made first and nothing restored unless the file changed; (b) a check that saves once under a file name of its own and loads it, the file deleted afterwards; (c) neither, and the user's own play answers, with the hooks writing to `game.log`. The monthly autosave is the reason for the rule; whether it also fires on a day inside a month has not been looked at.
4. **Territory in the diary:** every state that changes hands gets a line in the territory log; the main view shows capitals only. Or all, or none.
5. **Decorations for generals (E2):** five grades with neutral names and the game's default medal icons, or the game's own medals of each country by name (more work: about twenty national sets).
6. **Unit honours (E6):** written by the mod on its own when a capital falls, or only on the player's click; and whether an entry should offer a vanilla medal (`allow = yes`, at the game's price).
7. **Version:** 1.6 with the first page that ships.

## Risks

- **A record that outlives its subject.** The probe retired a general and annexed a country: both kept their names in the record, with no error (X34). Not seen: a general who dies in the field, and any of it after a save and a load.
- **One history entry a day per division** (X45). Unit honours cannot stack on a day, and how soon a division takes the next is not known.
- **Hooks run for the whole world.** A state changes hands thousands of times in a campaign. Every hook leaves at its first line unless a human country is involved; the cost is measured in E1 as X22 measured the loops.
- **Other mods.** A mod that replaces `on_actions` entries by file name does not remove the mod's hooks (the game merges them [LOAD-011]), but a total conversion may never fire some of them. Not tested until a compatibility checkpoint.
- **By Blood Alone.** Unit history and medals belong to that DLC. E6 is skipped without it (`has_dlc`).
